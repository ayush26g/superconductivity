### Theory

#### 1. Zero Electrical Resistance
In 1911, Heike Kamerlingh Onnes discovered that mercury loses all electrical resistance when cooled to 4.2 K. The temperature at which this transition occurs is known as the **Critical Temperature ($T_c$)**.

#### 2. The Meissner Effect
In 1933, Walther Meissner and Robert Ochsenfeld demonstrated that superconductors are more than just ideal conductors. When cooled below $T_c$ in the presence of an external magnetic field ($H$), the superconductor completely expels the internal magnetic flux density ($B$):

$$B = \mu_0 (H + M) = 0$$

This yields ideal diamagnetism with magnetic susceptibility:

$$\chi = \frac{M}{H} = -1$$

#### 3. Magnetic Levitation
When a permanent magnet is brought near a superconducting material below its $T_c$, screening surface currents are induced that produce an opposing magnetic field. This creates a repulsive force that balances gravitational force, causing the magnet to levitate stably.
