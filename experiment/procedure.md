# Procedure

## Objective
To observe the onset of zero electrical resistance and magnetic flux expulsion (Meissner effect) in a high-$T_c$ YBCO superconductor.

## Steps
1. Initialize Experiment: Start with the system at room temperature ($T = 300\text{ K}$). Observe the resting position of the YBCO pellet on the neodymium magnet base.
2. Observe Baseline Resistance: Note the resistance value on the telemetry panel ($R \approx 10\ \Omega$) and locate the tracking dot on the $R$–$T$ curve.
3. Turn On Magnetic Field Lines: Verify that the 3D magnetic flux lines penetrate directly through the normal-state pellet.
4. Pour Liquid Nitrogen ($LN_2$): Click the Pour LN₂ (77 K) button. Observe the continuous decline of temperature toward $77\text{ K}$.
5. Analyze the Critical Transition ($T_c = 93\text{ K}$):
   * Watch the resistance abruptly plummet to $0\ \Omega$ at $93\text{ K}$.
   * Observe the expulsion of the magnetic flux lines as they bow and curve outward around the specimen.
   * Observe stable magnetic levitation and flux-pinning rotation above the magnet.
6. Heating / Reset: Click Reset (300 K) to allow thermal energy to break Cooper pairs, watching the sample settle back onto the magnet as normal resistance returns.
